# Pleroma and Altermath: a careful analogy

**Pleroma** is Greek for "fullness." In strands of early Gnostic thought, notably Valentinian traditions, it represents the divine fullness or realm of Aeons. Complementary pairs of Aeons are called **syzygies**. The sources are varied and do not all give an identical map.

This is a **historical theological idea**, not a known computational architecture.

## The analogy proposed here

| Historical metaphor | Possible software interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Pleroma, the fullness | The connected whole considered at one level |
| Aeons, distinguishable emanations | Individual bounded subsystems or bubbles |
| Syzygies, paired complementary Aeons | Relations, compatibility and reciprocal exchange |
| Boundary and separation motifs | Permission-controlled access between domains |
| Return to fullness | A speculative analogy for re-integration or restored connectivity |

This table expresses a creative comparison, **not** a claim that ancient authors described networking, quantum mechanics or a sixth pentagram vertex.

## Where Altermath fits

An **Altermath Point** is a single consequential relation, while a **Pleroma-like whole** is the total connected pattern of many relations. In a recursively nested system, any connected whole may be represented as a bubble in a larger network. Multiple junctions can form, so the whole has no necessary single center.

The next engineering question is whether this representation is more useful than ordinary graphs in a measurable application. This repo implements a first graph simulation to make that question testable.
